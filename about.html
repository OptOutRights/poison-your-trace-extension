How Poison your Trace works

Poison your Trace makes it harder for websites to recognise you and stitch everything you do into a single profile. Here is what that means in plain terms, and exactly what it hides.

How it works

Websites recognise you in two main ways. First, with cookies: small files a site leaves in your browser so it remembers you next time. Second, with your browser's fingerprint: the mix of small details about your device (your screen size, time zone, graphics card, and dozens more) that together make you stand out from the crowd. When sites can share cookies and read the same fingerprint, they can join everything you do into one profile.

Poison your Trace breaks both. It keeps each site in its own box, so cookies from one site can't follow you to another. And it turns on Firefox's built-in protection that smooths over those telltale details, so your browser looks like a lot of other people's instead of like only you.

It hides you in a crowd. It does not disguise you. Giving each site a random, made-up device would just create a new one-of-a-kind label. Instead, this makes your browser report the same ordinary details that many people already report, so you blend in and nothing points clearly at you.

What it changes

Each site in its own box

Every website gets its own private space for cookies and logins. What you do on one site stays sealed off from every other site.

A browser that looks ordinary

Firefox's built-in protection replaces the details that make your device unique with common ones that many people share, so you blend into the crowd.

What it turns on

When you switch it on, the extension turns on Firefox's built-in fingerprint protection and a few privacy settings, and starts giving every site its own box. Everything is on by default. If a site misbehaves, the popup's Details panel lets you turn any single protection off.

If one site keeps breaking, use Pause here in the popup to add it to your list of paused sites. On a paused site the extension stops putting it in its own box. One thing to know: the "look ordinary" protection is a single Firefox-wide setting, not per-site, so pausing it also eases it for your other tabs while the paused tab is the one in front.

  • Make your browser look ordinary Smooths over the details that make your device unique (screen, time zone, graphics card, and more) toward values many people share.
  • Each site in its own box Every site gets its own private space for cookies and logins, so one site can't see what you do on another.
  • Keep your IP address from leaking Stops video and voice calls from revealing your real address.
  • Block known trackers Blocks companies known for following you, on every site.
  • Stop silent click reports Keeps the links you click from quietly reporting back to other companies.
  • Don't contact sites early Stops Firefox from reaching out to sites before you actually open them.

What it does not hide

  • Your IP address. Think of this as your internet return address. The extension never leaks it, but it can't change the address a site sees when you connect. For that, use a VPN or Tor alongside it.
  • Which system you use. Sites can still tell whether you are on Mac, Windows or Linux. Everything else in that part of the label is already the same for everyone on your version of Firefox.
Poison your Trace works entirely on your device. It makes no third-party calls to look anything up about you.
