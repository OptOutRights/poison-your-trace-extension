How Poison your Trace works

Poison your Trace stops your separate activities from being joined into one identity. Here is what that means in plain terms, and exactly what it hides.

How it works

Websites recognise you in two ways: the cookies they store in your browser, and your browser's fingerprint — the mix of small details (your screen, time zone, graphics card, and more) that together single out your device. When several sites can read the same fingerprint and share cookies, they can join everything you do into one profile.

Poison your Trace breaks both links:

It uniformizes, it does not randomize. A random value per site would just be a new unique label. Everyone running the extension shows the same common profile (Windows 10 · Firefox 128 · UTC · en-US), so the crowd blends together and the signals point at no one in particular.

What it changes

A container per site

Each website gets its own sealed cookie jar, so a login or tracker on one site cannot follow you to another.

One shared fingerprint

Instead of your unique device signature, every site sees the same common profile — you blend into the crowd.

A burner email

Signup forms get a throwaway address like amber.otter42@example.invalid, a different one per site so they cannot be linked.

What gets uniformized

When enabled, these fingerprint signals are reshaped toward one shared profile. The popup shows each one for the current tab, as its real value "to" the shared value.

  • Navigator The basics your browser announces: its name and version, your operating system, and your languages.
  • Screen Your screen's size, resolution, and colour depth.
  • Timezone Your time zone and clock offset, which hint at where in the world you are.
  • Canvas A hidden image your browser draws; tiny hardware quirks make it unique to your device. It is neutralized to a shared value.
  • WebGL The name of your graphics card and driver, exposed when a page renders 3D.
  • Plugins The list of browser plugins and file types your browser reports supporting.
  • Audio A hidden sound your browser processes; like canvas, its quirks identify your device. It is neutralized.
  • Request headers Labels attached to every request (the User-Agent and Accept-Language), sent before any page script even runs.

What it does not hide

  • Your IP address. The extension shows no IP and never looks one up, but it cannot change the address a site sees when your browser connects. Use a VPN or Tor for that.
  • Email is a burner only. The alias lives at a domain that can never receive mail, so it hides your real address on signup forms but is not an inbox.
  • Font metrics are not covered yet.
Poison your Trace works entirely on your device. It makes no third-party calls to look anything up about you.
