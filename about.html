How Poison your Trace works

Poison your Trace stops your separate activities from being joined into one identity. Here is what that means in plain terms, and exactly what it hides.

How it works

Websites recognise you in two ways: the cookies they store in your browser, and your browser's fingerprint: the mix of small details (your screen, time zone, graphics card, and more) that together make your device unique and tractable. When several sites can read the same fingerprint and share cookies, they can join everything you do into one profile.

Poison your Trace breaks both links: it gives every site its own sealed container so cookies cannot follow you, and it turns on Firefox's own fingerprint protection so your browser reports the same common values as everyone else running it.

It hides you in a crowd, it does not randomize. A random value per site would just be a new unique label. The extension turns on Firefox's own protection, which makes your browser report common values that many people share, so the crowd blends together and the signals point at no one in particular.

What it changes

A container per site

Each website gets its own sealed cookie jar, so a login or tracker on one site cannot follow you to another.

One shared fingerprint

Firefox's own protection makes every site see common values instead of your unique device signature. You are hidden in the crowd.

A burner email

Right click any email field to fill it with a throwaway address like amber.otter42@example.invalid, a different one per site so they cannot be linked.

What it turns on

When enabled, the extension turns on Firefox's own fingerprint protection plus a few privacy settings, and gives every site its own container. Everything defaults on; the popup's Details panel lets you turn any single protection off if a site misbehaves.

  • Fingerprint protection Firefox reshapes what a site can measure (canvas, screen, timezone, processor cores, audio and more) toward values that many users share.
  • Containers Each site gets its own sealed cookie jar, so a login or tracker on one site cannot follow you to another.
  • WebRTC Stops calls from leaking your real or local IP address.
  • Tracking protection Blocks known trackers on every site, always.
  • Hyperlink auditing Stops links from silently pinging a third party when you click.
  • Network prediction Stops the browser from reaching out to sites before you ask.
  • Burner email Right click any email field to fill it with a throwaway address, a different one per site so they cannot be linked.

What it does not hide

  • Your IP address. The extension shows no IP and never looks one up, but it cannot change the address a site sees when your browser connects. Use a VPN or Tor for that.
  • Email is a burner only. The alias lives at a domain that can never receive mail, so it hides your real address but is not an inbox. For a real signup that needs to receive mail, use Firefox Relay.
  • Your platform stays visible. The browser still reports whether you are on Mac, Windows or Linux. Everything else in that label is already the same for everyone on your Firefox version.
Poison your Trace works entirely on your device. It makes no third-party calls to look anything up about you.
