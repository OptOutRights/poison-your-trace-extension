How Poison your Trace works

Poison your Trace makes it harder for websites to recognise you and stitch everything you do into a single profile.

How it works

Websites recognise you in two main ways. First, with cookies: small files a site leaves in your browser so it remembers you next time. Second, with your browser's fingerprint: the mix of small details about your device (your screen size, time zone, graphics card, and dozens more) that together make you stand out from the crowd. When sites can share cookies and read the same fingerprint, they can join everything you do into one profile.

Poison your Trace helps breaking both. It keeps each site in its own jar, so cookies from one site can't follow you to another. And it turns on Firefox's built-in protections that smooth over your browsing information. Your browser looks like many other people instead of like only you.

The parameters that you can select

When you switch it on, the extension turns on Firefox's built-in fingerprint protection and a few privacy settings. It also starts giving every site its own jar. Everything is on by default. Each protection has its own tick box in the popup's panel, so if a site misbehaves you can partly turn off the extension.

If one site keeps breaking, disable the extension and feel free to contact. We will do our best to improve the extension.

  • Make your browser look ordinary Turns on Firefox's Resist Fingerprinting (RFP): it reports common, shared values for the details that make your device unique (screen size, time zone, graphics card, number of CPU cores, and more). Source: MDN privacy.websites.resistFingerprinting ↗
  • Each site in its own jar Puts every site in its own Firefox container, a separate cookie and storage jar. So one site can't see what you do on another. Source: Firefox Containers ↗
  • Keep your IP address from leaking Sets WebRTC to disable_non_proxied_udp, so a video or voice call can't quietly reveal your real or local address through a STUN request. Source: MDN privacy.network.webRTCIPHandlingPolicy ↗
  • Block known trackers Forces Firefox's Enhanced Tracking Protection to "always", blocking companies on Firefox's known-tracker list in every window. Source: Enhanced Tracking Protection ↗
  • Stop silent click reports Disables hyperlink auditing, the hidden <a ping> beacon that fires a background report to a third party when you click certain links. Source: MDN privacy.websites.hyperlinkAuditingEnabled ↗
  • Don't contact sites early Turns off network prediction, so Firefox stops pre-resolving DNS, pre-connecting and prefetching sites before you actually open them. Source: MDN privacy.network.networkPredictionEnabled ↗

Why some pages may look or behave differently

When the extension is doing its job, a few things change on the pages you visit. These are the protection working, not a broken page.

  • Noisy images Some pictures, graphics, or charts may look noise. That is the fingerprint protection reshaping what the page is allowed to read from your device, so you blend in with the crowd. If you need to send a video or an image, we highly recommend disabling the extension!
  • A site that opens logged out A site may open with nothing remembered, as if it were your first visit. That is the per-site box starting clean, so this site can't be matched to what you do anywhere else. Recent updates should have reduced this case of failure.
  • Occasional sign-in friction Now and then a site may ask you to sign in again or stumble on a login. If a site keeps misbehaving, disable the extension for the log in phase, then activate it again.

What it does not hide

  • Your IP address. The extension never leaks it, but it can't change the address a site sees when you connect. For that, use a VPN or Tor alongside it.
  • Which system you use. Sites can still tell whether you are on Mac, Windows or Linux. Everything else in that part of the label is already the same for everyone on your version of Firefox.
Poison your Trace works entirely on your device. It makes no third-party calls to look anything up about you.
